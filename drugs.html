💊 PHARMACOLOGY

Drug Cards

High-yield adult anaesthesia doses. Dose must always be checked against patient factors and the current product information.

Induction

Propofol

Adult IV induction: 1.5–2.5 mg/kg, titrated to effect; lower doses are commonly required in elderly/frail or haemodynamically compromised patients.

Mechanism: potentiates GABA-A receptor-mediated inhibition. Major concerns include dose-related hypotension and respiratory depression/apnoea.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting 6th ed.; current prescribing information.
NMBA

Rocuronium

Routine intubation: 0.6 mg/kg IV. Rapid-sequence intubation: 1.0–1.2 mg/kg IV may be used when clinically appropriate.

Non-depolarizing aminosteroid NMBA. Duration increases with larger doses. Reversal with sugammadex is dose-dependent on block depth.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting 6th ed.; current prescribing information.
NMBA reversal

Sugammadex

2 mg/kg for moderate block; 4 mg/kg for deep block; 16 mg/kg for immediate reversal after 1.2 mg/kg rocuronium in the indicated situation.

Selective relaxant binding agent that encapsulates aminosteroid NMBAs and reduces free plasma concentration.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting 6th ed.; current prescribing information.
Opioid

Fentanyl

IV dosing is titrated to the clinical objective and patient context; avoid treating a single “viva dose” as a universal dose for all patients.

Potent μ-opioid receptor agonist. Key adverse effects include respiratory depression, bradycardia and chest-wall rigidity with rapid/high-dose administration.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting 6th ed.
Neuromuscular blocker

Succinylcholine

Adult IV intubating dose: 1–1.5 mg/kg.

Depolarizing NMBA. Important contraindication/risk contexts include hyperkalaemia risk states, major burns/denervation after the relevant interval, certain neuromuscular disorders, and malignant hyperthermia susceptibility.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting 6th ed.; current prescribing information.
Anticholinesterase

Neostigmine

Use with an antimuscarinic and titrate to residual block; do not treat a deep block as if it were minimal residual block.

Increases acetylcholine at the NMJ by inhibiting acetylcholinesterase. Effect is limited by the depth of neuromuscular block and ceiling effect.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting 6th ed.
