Today's Knockout

Why does propofol cause hypotension?

Primarily through reduced systemic vascular resistance, venodilation and attenuation of sympathetic compensatory responses.

Knockout Pearl:

Propofol decreases sympathetic tone and causes arteriolar and venous vasodilation → ↓ SVR and ↓ venous return → hypotension.

Pharmacology

Succinylcholine

Rapid onset because it reaches the neuromuscular junction rapidly and acts as a depolarizing agonist.

Onset is approximately 30–60 seconds after IV administration. Viva: Why is onset faster than rocuronium?
Physiology

FRC matters

Preoxygenation increases the oxygen reservoir mainly by replacing nitrogen in the FRC with oxygen.

The functional residual capacity acts as an oxygen reservoir during apnoea. More FRC → greater oxygen reservoir → longer safe apnoea time.
Airway

Can't intubate, can't oxygenate

This is a time-critical airway emergency — don't persist indefinitely with repeated attempts.

Recognise failure early, declare the emergency, optimise oxygenation and proceed according to the applicable difficult-airway algorithm.
Critical Care

Sudden loss of ETCO₂

Think immediately about disconnection, airway problems, circulatory collapse and equipment failure.

A sudden abrupt loss of ETCO₂ can occur with disconnection, oesophageal intubation, complete airway obstruction, cardiac arrest or pulmonary embolism, among other causes.
Physiology

Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction

The pulmonary circulation constricts vessels supplying poorly ventilated alveoli.

HPV diverts pulmonary blood flow away from poorly ventilated regions toward better ventilated alveoli, helping reduce V/Q mismatch.
Pharmacology

Sugammadex

It reverses aminosteroid neuromuscular blockade by encapsulating the steroidal NMBA molecule.

Sugammadex forms a tight complex with rocuronium and other aminosteroid neuromuscular blockers, reducing free drug concentration at the NMJ.

📚 Notes

Concepts worth understanding before trying to memorise them.

Cardiovascular

Mitral Stenosis

Understand the haemodynamic consequences of reduced mitral valve area and the importance of heart rate, rhythm and pulmonary vascular resistance.

Core goals:
  • Maintain sinus rhythm.
  • Avoid tachycardia.
  • Maintain adequate preload without volume overload.
  • Avoid increases in PVR.
  • Maintain contractility and systemic perfusion.
Respiratory

V/Q Mismatch

The most common mechanism of hypoxaemia in many clinical pulmonary disorders.

Low V/Q regions receive blood flow but insufficient ventilation. High V/Q regions receive ventilation but relatively less perfusion.
Airway

Preoxygenation

The goal isn't simply increasing SpO₂ — it is increasing the oxygen reserve available during apnoea.

Effective preoxygenation reduces alveolar nitrogen and increases the oxygen fraction within the FRC.

🧠 Viva Classics

The questions that keep coming back.

Viva #001

Why does N₂O expand a pneumothorax?

N₂O diffuses into closed gas spaces faster than nitrogen diffuses out, increasing the volume and/or pressure of the gas space.
Viva #002

What determines cerebral perfusion pressure?

CPP = MAP − ICP When CVP exceeds ICP, the effective downstream pressure may instead be influenced by CVP.
Viva #003

Why is etomidate haemodynamically stable?

Etomidate generally produces less cardiovascular depression than propofol, with relatively preserved sympathetic tone and baroreflex function.
Viva #004

What is the difference between shunt and dead space?

Shunt: Perfusion without adequate ventilation.

Dead space: Ventilation without adequate perfusion.
Viva #005

What is the most important determinant of MAC?

Age is one of the most important clinically relevant determinants of MAC, with MAC generally decreasing with increasing age.
Viva #006

Why does spinal anaesthesia cause hypotension?

Sympathetic blockade causes arterial and venous vasodilation → reduced SVR and venous capacitance changes → reduced venous return and cardiac output.
