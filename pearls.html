Pharmacology
NMBA

Succinylcholine

IV intubating dose: 1–1.5 mg/kg in adults.

Depolarizing NMBA with rapid onset (30–60s) and brief duration (5–10 mins). Produces fasciculations followed by flaccid paralysis. Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting's Pharmacology & Physiology, 6th ed.
Pharmacology
Reversal

Sugammadex

Dose according to depth of rocuronium or vecuronium block.

2 mg/kg for moderate block (reappearance of T2); 4 mg/kg for deep block (1–2 post-tetanic counts); 16 mg/kg for immediate reversal 3 minutes after 1.2 mg/kg rocuronium. Reference: Stoelting's Pharmacology & Physiology in Anesthetic Practice, 6th ed.
Physiology
Oxygenation

FRC as an Oxygen Reservoir

Preoxygenation replaces alveolar nitrogen with oxygen to prolong safe apnoea time.

The primary goal is denitrogenation and maximisation of the functional residual capacity (FRC) oxygen store (~2100 mL in a 70kg adult breathing 100% O₂), not merely achieving an SpO₂ of 100%. Reference: West's Respiratory Physiology; Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.
Airway
Emergency

Can't Intubate, Can't Oxygenate (CICO)

Recognise failure early and immediately progress to emergency front-of-neck access.

Declare CICO emergency. Call for senior help, attempt 100% O₂ rescue via supraglottic airway / facemask, and if uncorrected, perform emergency scalpel-bougie-tube cricothyroidotomy immediately. Reference: Difficult Airway Society (DAS) Guidelines.
Cardiac
Valvular Disease

Mitral Stenosis: Rate Matters

Tachycardia shortens diastole and critically impairs LV filling across a stenotic valve.

Haemodynamic goals: Maintain sinus rhythm, control ventricular rate (60–75 bpm), ensure adequate but cautious preload, preserve contractility, and avoid acute elevations in pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR). Reference: Kaplan's Cardiac Anesthesia, 8th ed.
Critical Care
Monitoring

Sudden Loss of ETCO₂

Rapidly differentiate equipment disconnections from catastrophic circulatory failure.

Rule out circuit disconnection, endotracheal tube displacement/obstruction, sensor failure, massive pulmonary embolism, and sudden circulatory arrest. Check pulse and blood pressure immediately. Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.
