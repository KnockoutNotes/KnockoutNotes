One concept. One takeaway. Exact numbers where they matter.

Pharmacology

Succinylcholine

IV intubating dose: 1–1.5 mg/kg in adults.

Depolarizing NMBA with rapid onset. It produces fasciculations followed by flaccid paralysis.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting 6th ed.
Pharmacology

Sugammadex

Dose according to depth of rocuronium/vecuronium block.

2 mg/kg for moderate block; 4 mg/kg for deep block; 16 mg/kg for immediate reversal after a 1.2 mg/kg rocuronium dose in the relevant setting.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Stoelting 6th ed.; current product prescribing information.
Physiology

FRC as an oxygen reservoir

Preoxygenation replaces alveolar nitrogen with oxygen and increases oxygen reserve during apnoea.

The goal is denitrogenation and maximisation of oxygen stores, not merely achieving an SpO₂ of 100%.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Barash, Cullen & Stoelting's Clinical Anesthesia, 9th ed.
Airway

Can't intubate, can't oxygenate

Recognise failure early and move to the rescue pathway rather than repeating unsuccessful attempts indefinitely.

Priorities are oxygenation, declaration of the emergency, optimisation of rescue attempts and progression through the applicable difficult-airway algorithm.Reference: Current difficult-airway guidance; Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.
Cardiac

Mitral stenosis: rate matters

Tachycardia shortens diastole and reduces LV filling across a stenotic mitral valve.

Aim for controlled heart rate, sinus rhythm when possible, adequate but not excessive preload, and avoidance of acute rises in pulmonary vascular resistance.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Barash, Cullen & Stoelting's Clinical Anesthesia, 9th ed.
Critical Care

Sudden loss of ETCO₂

Think patient, airway, circuit and circulation immediately.

Consider disconnection, airway obstruction, oesophageal intubation, sampling/circuit failure, pulmonary embolism and abrupt reduction in pulmonary blood flow such as cardiac arrest.Reference: Miller's Anesthesia, 10th ed.; Barash, Cullen & Stoelting's Clinical Anesthesia, 9th ed.
