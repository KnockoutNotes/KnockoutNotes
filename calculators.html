Paediatric Airway Score

COPUR Paediatric Airway Score (Colorado Score)

Specialized paediatric airway risk scoring tool assessing Chin, Opening, Previous intubations/OSA, Uvula, Range of motion, plus phenotypic modifiers.

Easy Intubation
5 Points
Cormack-Lehane Grade 1 Expected
5–7: Easy | 8–10: More difficult (laryngeal pressure) | 11–13: Difficult | 14–15: Advanced airway | ≥ 16: Dangerous
Conventional direct laryngoscopy or standard paediatric videolaryngoscope indicated.
Source: Petrov et al. (J Clin Med 2024;13:4294) ↗

Functional Reserve

METs Functional Capacity

Preoperative functional capacity assessment derived from ACC/AHA and ESAIC guidelines.

Moderate Reserve
4 – 6 METs
1 MET = 3.5 mL O₂ / kg / min (resting metabolic rate)
Sufficient functional capacity for most low-to-intermediate risk surgeries without further invasive cardiac testing if clinically stable.
Source: ACC/AHA Guidelines ↗

Airway & Sleep

STOP-Bang OSA Screening

Validated questionnaire identifying patients at risk of obstructive sleep apnoea for perioperative airway and monitoring planning.

Low Risk of OSA
0 / 8 Yes
Low: 0–2 | Intermediate: 3–4 | High: 5–8 or STOP score ≥ 2 + (Male / BMI > 35 / Neck)
Screening questionnaire only. Not a formal diagnostic polysomnogram. High-risk patients require opioid caution and airway vigilance.
Source: Chung et al. (Anesthesiology 2008) ↗

Primary acidaemia. Severe negative base excess confirms profound metabolic acid accumulation.

Metabolic Acidosis

Primary Disturbance

Primary Metabolic Acidosis
Direction: pH < 7.35 with HCO₃⁻ 11 mEq/L (Normal 22–26)

Both pH and HCO₃⁻ are reduced in the same direction, defining a primary metabolic acidosis.

Step 3 (Winter's)
Appropriate

Expected Compensation

Expected PaCO₂: 22.5 – 26.5 mmHg
Winter's Formula: Expected PaCO₂ = (1.5 × [HCO₃⁻]) + 8 ± 2

Measured PaCO₂ (26 mmHg) matches expected Winter's compensation, confirming simple respiratory response without secondary respiratory failure.

Step 4
High Anion Gap

Serum Anion Gap & Albumin

AG: 29.0 mEq/L
AG = Na⁺ - (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻) | Figge Corr = AG + 2.5 × (4.0 - Alb)

High anion gap (> 12 mEq/L) signals unmeasured anions: ketoacids, lactate, toxins, or uraemic acids.

Step 5
Pure HAGMA

Delta Gap & Delta Ratio

Ratio: 1.31 | ΔAG: 17.0 | ΔHCO₃: 13.0
ΔAG = AG - 12 | ΔHCO₃ = 24 - HCO₃⁻ | Ratio = ΔAG ÷ ΔHCO₃

Delta ratio between 0.8 and 2.0 indicates an uncomplicated High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (DKA or lactic acidosis).

Step 6 (Oxygenation)
P/F: 405 (Normal)

P/F Ratio & Alveolar Gradient

P/F: 404.8 | Expected PaO₂: 92.0 mmHg
P/F = PaO₂ ÷ FiO₂ | PAO₂ = [FiO₂ × (760 - 47)] - (PaCO₂ ÷ 0.8)

Normal oxygenation on room air. A-a gradient is within normal age-adjusted limits.

🩺 Integrated Diagnostic Impression

Primary High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA) with Appropriate Winter's Respiratory Compensation
No secondary respiratory disorder or concurrent non-gap acidosis detected. Oxygen transfer is intact without acute pulmonary shunting.
References: Narins & Emmett (Medicine 1980); Albert & Winters (Ann Intern Med 1967); Berlin ARDS Definition (JAMA 2012) ↗
