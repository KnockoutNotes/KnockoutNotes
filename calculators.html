Primary Metabolic Acidosis

Step 3 (Winter's)
Appropriate

Expected Compensation

Expected PaCO₂: 22.5 – 26.5 mmHg
Winter's Formula: Expected PaCO₂ = (1.5 × [HCO₃⁻]) + 8 ± 2

Measured PaCO₂ (26 mmHg) matches expected Winter's compensation, confirming simple respiratory response without secondary respiratory failure.

Step 4
High Anion Gap

Serum Anion Gap & Albumin

AG: 29.0 mEq/L
AG = Na⁺ - (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻) | Figge Corr = AG + 2.5 × (4.0 - Alb)

High anion gap (> 12 mEq/L) signals unmeasured anions: ketoacids, lactate, toxins, or uraemic acids.

Step 5
Pure HAGMA

Delta Gap & Delta Ratio

Ratio: 1.31 | ΔAG: 17.0 | ΔHCO₃: 13.0
ΔAG = AG - 12 | ΔHCO₃ = 24 - HCO₃⁻ | Ratio = ΔAG ÷ ΔHCO₃

Delta ratio between 0.8 and 2.0 indicates an uncomplicated High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (DKA or lactic acidosis).

Step 6 (Oxygenation)
P/F: 405 (Normal)

P/F Ratio & Alveolar Gradient

P/F: 404.8 | Expected PaO₂: 92.0 mmHg
P/F = PaO₂ ÷ FiO₂ | PAO₂ = [FiO₂ × (760 - 47)] - (PaCO₂ ÷ 0.8)

Normal oxygenation on room air. A-a gradient is within normal age-adjusted limits.

🩺 Integrated Diagnostic Impression

Primary High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (HAGMA) with Appropriate Winter's Respiratory Compensation
No secondary respiratory disorder or concurrent non-gap acidosis detected. Oxygen transfer is intact without acute pulmonary shunting.
References: Narins & Emmett (Medicine 1980); Albert & Winters (Ann Intern Med 1967); Berlin ARDS Definition (JAMA 2012) ↗
