Paediatric Airway Score

COPUR Paediatric Airway Score (Colorado Score)

Specialized paediatric airway risk scoring tool assessing Chin, Opening, Previous intubations/OSA, Uvula, Range of motion, plus phenotypic modifiers.

Easy Intubation
5 Points
Cormack-Lehane Grade 1 Expected
5–7: Easy | 8–10: More difficult (laryngeal pressure) | 11–13: Difficult | 14–15: Advanced airway | ≥ 16: Dangerous
Conventional direct laryngoscopy or standard paediatric videolaryngoscope indicated.
Source: Petrov et al. (J Clin Med 2024;13:4294) ↗

Parkland Burn Resuscitation

Calculates crystalloid fluid requirements in the first 24 hours for patients with significant 2nd and 3rd degree burns.

%
hrs
mL
Baxter / Parkland Formula
8,400 mL / 24h
4 mL × Weight (kg) × % TBSA
1st 8h window (remaining 6.0 hrs): 3,200 mL remaining → 533 mL/hr.
Next 16h window: 4,200 mL total → 263 mL/hr.
Fluid: Ringer's Lactate / Hartmann's solution. Target adult urine output: 0.5 mL/kg/h.
Source: Baxter & Shires (1968) ↗
Perioperative Assessment Risk Stratification
Airway Risk Score

Wilson Risk Score (Difficult Intubation)

Validated 5-factor risk scoring system predicting difficult direct laryngoscopy based on weight, neck mobility, buck teeth, jaw subluxation, and retrognathia.

Easy Intubation
0 / 10 Points
0–1: Easy | 2–3: Possibly difficult intubation | ≥ 4: Often difficult intubation
Low likelihood of difficult direct laryngoscopy. Standard airway management indicated.
Source: Wilson ME et al. (Br J Anaesth 1988) ↗

Functional Reserve

METs Functional Capacity

Preoperative functional capacity assessment derived from ACC/AHA and ESAIC guidelines.

Moderate Reserve
4 – 6 METs
1 MET = 3.5 mL O₂ / kg / min (resting metabolic rate)
Sufficient functional capacity for most low-to-intermediate risk surgeries without further invasive cardiac testing if clinically stable.
Source: ACC/AHA Guidelines ↗
Cardiorespiratory Score

Duke Activity Status Index (DASI)

Validated 12-item questionnaire quantifying functional capacity, peak VO₂, and 30-day perioperative cardiac risk (METS Study, Lancet 2018).

DASI Score
0.0 / 58.2
Peak VO₂: 9.6 mL/kg/min
Est. METs: 2.7 METs
Peak VO₂ = (0.43 × DASI) + 9.6 | Estimated METs = Peak VO₂ ÷ 3.5
Select the activities the patient can perform without significant chest pain or breathlessness.
Source: Hlatky et al. (1989) & METS Study (Lancet 2018) ↗
Airway & Sleep

STOP-Bang OSA Screening

Validated questionnaire identifying patients at risk of obstructive sleep apnoea for perioperative airway and monitoring planning.

Low Risk of OSA
0 / 8 Yes
Low: 0–2 | Intermediate: 3–4 | High: 5–8 or STOP score ≥ 2 + (Male / BMI > 35 / Neck)
Screening questionnaire only. Not a formal diagnostic polysomnogram. High-risk patients require opioid caution and airway vigilance.
Source: Chung et al. (Anesthesiology 2008) ↗

Renal Clearance

Creatinine Clearance (Cockcroft–Gault)

Estimates creatinine clearance for renal drug dosing adjustments in adult patients.

yrs
kg
cm
Normal Clearance
72.9 mL/min
CrCl = [(140 - Age) × Weight] ÷ [72 × SCr] × (0.85 if female)
Mildly decreased (60–89 mL/min). Not interchangeable with CKD-EPI eGFR. Adjust dose for renally excreted drugs.
Source: Cockcroft & Gault (1976) ↗

Serum Anion Gap

Evaluates metabolic acidosis and unmeasured anions, with optional Figge albumin correction.

mEq/L
g/dL
Normal Anion Gap
14.0 mEq/L

Normal uncorrected reference range: 4 – 12 mEq/L (without potassium). Excludes K⁺ by convention.
Source: Figge et al. (Crit Care Med 1998) ↗
Acid–Base

Winter's Formula

Evaluates respiratory compensation in metabolic acidosis. Compares expected vs measured PaCO₂.

mEq/L
mmHg
Expected Compensation
24.0 – 28.0 mmHg
Expected PaCO₂ = (1.5 × [HCO₃⁻]) + 8 ± 2 mmHg
Measured PaCO₂ (26 mmHg) matches expected range. Demonstrates appropriate simple respiratory compensation.
Source: Albert & Winters (1967) ↗

Detects mixed high anion gap and normal anion gap acidoses or concurrent metabolic alkalosis.

mEq/L
g/dL
Pure HAGMA
