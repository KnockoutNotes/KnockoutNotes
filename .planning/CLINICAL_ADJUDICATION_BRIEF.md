## Topic 1: Pediatric Septic-Shock Hydrocortisone

### 1.1 Current Curriculum Presentation in KnockoutNotes
- **Topic ID / Module:** `paediatric-shock-critical-care` / Section: *Adrenal Insufficiency & Stress-Dose Corticosteroids in Children*
- **Current Claim:**
  - *Responsive Septic Shock:* Guidelines suggest AGAINST intravenous hydrocortisone when hemodynamic stability can be restored with adequate fluid resuscitation and vasoactive therapy.
  - *Persistent Shock Despite Fluids & Vasoactives:* Evidence is insufficient to issue a recommendation for or against corticosteroid treatment in pediatric septic shock that remains refractory to initial fluid and vasoactive therapy (unsettled clinical question).
  - *Adrenal Insufficiency:* Stress-dose corticosteroids SHOULD be administered in children with suspected or documented adrenal insufficiency presenting in septic shock (e.g., chronic systemic steroid therapy, congenital adrenal hyperplasia, hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis disease, or purpura fulminans / Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome).
  - *Guideline Boundary:* Surviving Sepsis Campaign guidelines do NOT endorse a specific steroid dose, infusion regimen, catecholamine threshold, or taper protocol in their formal recommendations.
  - *Educational Regimens (Non-SSC Pharmacology Sources):* Intermittent hydrocortisone 1.0 to 2.0 mg/kg/dose IV q6h (max adult dose 50 mg q6h / 200 mg/d) or 50 to 100 mg/m²/day continuous infusion from reference sources (Harriet Lane, PALS). Adult fixed 200 mg/day dosing is avoided due to the pediatric weight-based dosing principle. Tapering varies by institutional PICU protocol.

### 1.2 Applicable International Guidelines & Evidence Certainty
- **Surviving Sepsis Campaign International Guidelines for the Management of Septic Shock and Sepsis-Associated Organ Dysfunction in Children (pSSC 2020 / SCCM 2026):**
  - *Recommendation on Responsive Shock:* Suggests against IV hydrocortisone in children whose shock resolves with fluids and vasoactive therapy.
  - *Recommendation on Fluid- and Vasoactive-Refractory Shock:* The guideline panel found the evidence insufficient to issue a recommendation for or against hydrocortisone administration in pediatric septic shock that persists despite fluid resuscitation and escalating vasoactive therapy.
  - *Recommendation on Adrenal Insufficiency:* Recommends stress-dose corticosteroids for suspected or proven adrenal insufficiency.

### 1.3 Key Primary Literature & Reference Evidence
1. **Weiss SL, Peters MJ, Alhazzani W, et al.** *Surviving Sepsis Campaign International Guidelines for the Management of Septic Shock and Sepsis-Associated Organ Dysfunction in Children.* Pediatr Crit Care Med. 2020;21(2):e52–e106. DOI: [10.1097/PCC.0000000000002198](https://doi.org/10.1097/PCC.0000000000002198)
2. **Menon K, McNally JD, Choong K, et al.** *A Randomized Controlled Trial of Corticosteroids in Pediatric Septic Shock (SHIP Trial).* Crit Care Med. 2017;45(11):e1098–e1107. DOI: [10.1097/CCM.0000000000002621](https://doi.org/10.1097/CCM.0000000000002621)
3. **Johns Hopkins Hospital.** *The Harriet Lane Handbook: A Manual for Pediatric House Officers.* 23rd ed. Elsevier; 2024.
4. **American Heart Association.** *Pediatric Advanced Life Support (PALS) Provider Manual.* AHA; 2020.

### 1.4 Clinical Controversies & Areas of Uncertainty
- Intermittent IV bolus dosing (1–2 mg/kg q6h) versus continuous IV infusion (50–100 mg/m²/day) in terms of glycemic control, secondary infection risk, and hemodynamic rebound.
- Absence of validated serum cortisol or ACTH stimulation thresholds in acute, hyperdynamic pediatric septic shock.
- Lack of consensus on tapering duration (abrupt discontinuation upon pressor weaning vs 24–72 hour gradual taper).

### 1.5 Specific Questions for Clinician / Committee Adjudication
1. Does the unit endorse a specific vasoactive threshold (e.g., VIS > 15–20 or epinephrine equivalent > 0.2 mcg/kg/min) before initiating empiric stress-dose hydrocortisone in refractory pediatric shock?
2. Does the unit's local protocol mandate intermittent (q6h) or continuous infusion, and what maximum daily dose cap is enforced?
3. What duration and tapering schedule should be standard after vasopressor discontinuation?

### 1.6 Recommended Reviewer Specialty
- **Specialty:** Pediatric Intensive Care (PICU) Intensivist / Pediatric Critical Care Fellowship Director / Pediatric Clinical Pharmacologist.

## Topic 2: Selective Digestive Decontamination (SDD / SOD) & Antimicrobial Resistance

### 2.1 Current Curriculum Presentation in KnockoutNotes
- **Topic ID / Module:** `icu-severity-scores-bundles-ethics` / Section: *ICU Quality Bundles & Adverse Event Prevention*
- **Current Claim:**
  - *SSC 2026 Stance:* In mechanically ventilated adults in ICUs with a low prevalence of antimicrobial resistance, the Surviving Sepsis Campaign issues a conditional recommendation suggesting the use of SDD (**moderate-certainty evidence**).
  - *Modality Differentiation:* Clearly differentiates:
    1. **SDD:** 4-day systemic IV antibiotic prophylaxis + topical/enteral non-absorbable antimicrobial paste/suspension (colistin, tobramycin, amphotericin B/nystatin) q6h throughout ventilation.
    2. **SOD:** Topical paste applied to oropharynx alone (omitting IV antibiotics and gastric instillation).
    3. **Topical Antiseptics (Chlorhexidine):** Chemical biocide; scrutinized due to observational concerns regarding mucosal toxicity and excess mortality in non-cardiac ICUs.
    4. **Standard Non-Antibiotic VAE Bundles:** 30°–45° head elevation, subglottic secretion drainage, daily SAT/SBT weaning, cuff pressure (20–30 cmH2O).
  - *SuDDICU Trial Reporting:* Sourced accurately from *JAMA* 2022 (n=5,982): in-hospital mortality was 27.0% with SDD vs 29.1% with standard care (OR 0.91, 95% CI 0.82–1.02; p = 0.10); no statistically significant reduction in primary hospital mortality, though secondary VAP and bacteremia rates decreased.
  - *High-AMR Setting Framing:* In critical care units with high endemic rates of multidrug-resistant organisms (MDROs: CRE, CRAB, ESBL), routine broad enteral antibiotic decontamination is avoided as an antimicrobial stewardship consideration to avoid selective pressure for pan-drug and colistin resistance.

### 2.2 Applicable International Guidelines & Evidence Certainty
- **Surviving Sepsis Campaign International Guidelines for Management of Sepsis and Septic Shock 2026:**
  - *Recommendation:* In mechanically ventilated adults with sepsis or septic shock in ICUs with a low prevalence of antimicrobial resistance, suggests the use of selective digestive decontamination (conditional recommendation, **moderate-certainty evidence**).

### 2.3 Key Primary Literature & Reference Evidence
1. **Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026 Panel.** *Surviving Sepsis Campaign: International Guidelines for Management of Sepsis and Septic Shock 2026.* Intensive Care Med. 2026. DOI: [10.1007/s00134-026-08361-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00134-026-08361-1)
2. **The SuDDICU Collaborative Group.** *Effect of Selective Decontamination of the Digestive Tract on In-Hospital Mortality in Critically Ill Patients: A Randomized Clinical Trial.* JAMA. 2022;328(19):1911–1921. DOI: [10.1001/jama.2022.19709](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2022.19709)
3. **de Smet AM, Kluytmans JA, Cooper BS, et al.** *Decontamination of the Digestive Tract and Oropharynx in ICU Patients.* N Engl J Med. 2009;360(1):20–31. DOI: [10.1056/NEJMoa0800394](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa0800394)
4. **Wittekamp BH, Plantinga NL, Cooper BS, et al.** *Decontamination Strategies and Bloodstream Infections with Antibiotic-Resistant Microorganisms in Ventilated Patients: A Randomized Clinical Trial.* JAMA. 2018;320(20):2087–2098. DOI: [10.1001/jama.2018.13765](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.13765)

### 2.4 Clinical Controversies & Areas of Uncertainty
- Definition of "low prevalence of antimicrobial resistance": international guidelines do not define an absolute mathematical cut-off for CRE, CRAB, or ESBL prevalence above which SDD harm exceeds benefit.
- Concerns regarding emergence of colistin/polymyxin resistance following enteral colistin decontamination in units with existing plasmid-mediated (mcr-1) or chromosomal resistance.
- Role of SOD vs SDD vs chlorhexidine oral hygiene in high-risk post-cardiac surgical vs general medical-surgical ICU cohorts.

### 2.5 Specific Questions for Clinician / Committee Adjudication
1. Does the institution / hospital antimicrobial stewardship committee (AMSC) authorize SDD or SOD in any mechanically ventilated patient cohort?
2. If SDD is considered in selected populations, what baseline unit microbiological surveillance thresholds (e.g., screening for colistin-resistant Gram-negatives or CRE colonization) must be in place?
3. What is the institutional policy regarding chlorhexidine oral care versus non-antiseptic oral cleansing protocols in mechanically ventilated adults?

### 2.6 Recommended Reviewer Specialty
- **Specialty:** Adult Intensive Care Intensivist / Hospital Infection Control Lead / Clinical Infectious Diseases Specialist / Antimicrobial Stewardship Chair.

## Topic 3: Extracorporeal Blood Purification & Hemoadsorption

### 3.1 Current Curriculum Presentation in KnockoutNotes
- **Topic ID / Module:** `ecco2r-cytokine-adsorption-blood-purification` / Section: *Clinical Trial Evidence & Guidelines for Blood Purification in Septic Shock: Surviving Sepsis Campaign Stance*
- **Current Claim:**
  - *Routine Blood Purification Techniques:* In adults with sepsis or septic shock, SSC 2026 suggests AGAINST the routine use of blood purification techniques, including hemoperfusion sorbent cartridges (CytoSorb, Jafron HA330), hemoadsorption, plasmapheresis, or high-volume hemofiltration outside randomized clinical trials (**conditional recommendation, very-low-certainty evidence**).
  - *Polymyxin B Hemoperfusion:* In adults with septic shock, SSC 2026 separately suggests AGAINST using polymyxin B hemoperfusion columns (**conditional recommendation, low-certainty evidence**).
  - *Surrogate vs Clinical Outcomes:* Sorbents reliably clear circulating cytokines (IL-6, TNF-alpha) or endotoxin, but randomized trials (EUPHRATES in *JAMA* 2018, CytoSorb-Sepsis in *Crit Care* 2017) show substantial uncertainty and have not demonstrated consistent, reproducible improvements in patient-centered endpoints (28-day mortality, organ failure-free days, ICU LOS).
  - *Clinical Safety Risks:*
    1. Non-selective antimicrobial adsorption (beta-lactams, vancomycin, aminoglycosides, colistin, linezolid) precipitating subtherapeutic exposure without aggressive therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM).
    2. Platelet consumption, leukopenia, and thrombocytopenia from direct synthetic polymer/fiber contact.
    3. Column thrombosis requiring systemic anticoagulation with associated bleeding risks.
    4. Albumin and coagulation factor depletion.
    5. Operational distinction from standard Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy (CRRT: CVVH/CVVHD/CVVHDF), which maintains established, life-saving indications for acute kidney injury and fluid/electrolyte crises (AEIOU criteria).

### 3.2 Applicable International Guidelines & Evidence Certainty
- **Surviving Sepsis Campaign International Guidelines for Management of Sepsis and Septic Shock 2026:**
  - *Blood Purification Broadly:* Suggests against routine use of blood purification techniques (conditional recommendation, **very-low-certainty evidence**).
  - *Polymyxin B Hemoperfusion Column:* Suggests against polymyxin B hemoperfusion (conditional recommendation, **low-certainty evidence**).

### 3.3 Key Primary Literature & Reference Evidence
1. **Surviving Sepsis Campaign 2026 Panel.** *Surviving Sepsis Campaign: International Guidelines for Management of Sepsis and Septic Shock 2026.* Intensive Care Med. 2026. DOI: [10.1007/s00134-026-08361-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00134-026-08361-1)
2. **Dellinger RP, Bagshaw SM, Antonelli M, et al.** *Effect of Targeted Polymyxin B Hemoperfusion on 28-Day Mortality in Patients With Septic Shock: The EUPHRATES Randomized Clinical Trial.* JAMA. 2018;320(14):1455–1463. DOI: [10.1001/jama.2018.14618](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.14618)
3. **Schädler D, Pausch C, Heise D, et al.** *The Effect of a Novel Extracorporeal Cytokine Adsorber (CytoSorb) in Patients with Septic Shock or Severe Sepsis: A Multicenter, Randomized, Controlled Study.* Crit Care. 2017;21(1):247. DOI: [10.1186/s13054-017-1850-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13054-017-1850-5)
4. **Schneider AG, Baldwin I, Fealy N, et al.** *Antimicrobial Clearance during Extracorporeal Cytokine Removal.* Blood Purif. 2021;50(3):320–328. DOI: [10.1159/000510842](https://doi.org/10.1159/000510842)

### 3.4 Clinical Controversies & Areas of Uncertainty
- Potential post-hoc subgroup benefits with polymyxin B hemoperfusion in patients with intermediate-high endotoxin activity assay levels (EAA 0.60–0.89), currently under prospective evaluation in the TIGRIS trial.
- Compassionate off-label deployment of sorbent cartridges in severe vasoplegic shock refractory to high-dose multimodal pressors.
- Pharmacokinetic unpredictability of critical antimicrobials during concurrent hemoadsorption when real-time TDM is unavailable.

### 3.5 Specific Questions for Clinician / Committee Adjudication
1. Does the critical care department permit off-label or compassionate use of cytokine adsorbers or polymyxin B hemoperfusion in refractory septic shock, and under what institutional review / clinical trial governance?
2. If deployed, what mandatory therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) and supplemental dosing protocols are required for beta-lactams and vancomycin?
3. What baseline platelet and coagulation thresholds restrict sorbent column use?

### 3.6 Recommended Reviewer Specialty
- **Specialty:** Critical Care Intensivist / Nephrologist (CRRT Lead) / Critical Care Clinical Pharmacist.
