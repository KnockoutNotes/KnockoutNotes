Emergency Protocol • Difficult Airway Society

CICO: Can't Intubate, Can't Oxygenate

Management of unanticipated difficult tracheal intubation in adults. Progress systematically through Plans A → B → C → D.

Source: Difficult Airway Society (DAS) 2025 Guidelines (British Journal of Anaesthesia)
⚡ 1. Immediate Actions
  • Declare Airway Emergency to Theatre Team
    Call explicitly for senior anaesthetic help, ENT/surgical support, and difficult airway trolley.
  • Ensure 100% Oxygen Delivery & Continuous Peroxygenation
    High-flow oxygen with verified circuit integrity and continuous apnoeic oxygenation (e.g. HFNO).
  • Confirm Full Neuromuscular Blockade
    Ensure complete paralysis before declaring failure of intubation or facemask ventilation.
📋 2. DAS Guideline Sequence
PLAN A • Tracheal Intubation Max 3 + 1 Attempts
  • Optimize position: Head and neck positioning (ramped if obese, sniffing position).
  • Universal Videolaryngoscopy: Routine first-line videolaryngoscopy (VL) recommended for all intubations to maximize first-pass success; use bougie or stylet.
  • Peroxygenation: Maintain continuous oxygen delivery throughout (apnoeic oxygenation / HFNO).
  • Relaxation: Ensure profound neuromuscular paralysis.
  • Limit attempts: Maximum 3 attempts (plus 1 by an experienced colleague). Maintain oxygenation between attempts.
PLAN B • SAD Oxygenation & Ventilation Max 3 Attempts
  • Device: 2nd generation Supraglottic Airway Device (SAD) strongly preferred.
  • Limit attempts: Maximum 3 attempts. Change size or device if failed.
  • If successful: Oxygenate, confirm capnography trace. Plan stop and think: Wake patient, intubate via SAD, proceed with SAD, or surgical tracheostomy.
PLAN C • Facemask Ventilation Final Attempt
  • Technique: 2-person facemask ventilation with oral (Guedel) and/or nasopharyngeal airway adjuncts.
  • Paralysis check: Verify complete neuromuscular block. (Reverse rocuronium/vecuronium with Sugammadex 16 mg/kg if waking the patient is immediately possible).
  • If ventilation impossible: DECLARE "CAN'T INTUBATE, CAN'T OXYGENATE" (CICO).
🚨 PLAN D • Emergency Front of Neck Access (eFONA) SCALPEL - BOUGIE - TUBE

CICO DECLARED: Immediate scalpel cricothyroidotomy. Do NOT delay for further non-surgical attempts.

1
Position & Laryngeal Handshake

Extend patient's neck fully. Stabilize larynx with non-dominant hand ("laryngeal handshake") to locate the cricothyroid membrane.

2
Scalpel Stab & Caudal Turn

Transverse stab incision through cricothyroid membrane using size 10 scalpel. Turn blade 90° so sharp edge points caudally (towards feet).

3
Bougie Insertion

Slide coudé-tip bougie into trachea alongside flat of scalpel blade into the lumen (aiming towards carina). Remove scalpel.

4
Railroad Cuffed 6.0 mm Tube

Railroad a cuffed 6.0 mm (or 5.0 mm) endotracheal tube over bougie into trachea. Inflate cuff, ventilate, and confirm with continuous waveform capnography.

⚠️ 3. What to Avoid
Pitfalls & Delays to Avoid in CICO
  • Do NOT make repeated intubation attempts beyond Plan A limits (worsens laryngeal trauma and bleeding).
  • Do NOT delay declaring CICO while waiting for secondary rescue equipment or ENT arrival.
  • Do NOT perform needle cricothyroidotomy if equipment or expertise for scalpel technique is ready (DAS 2015 prioritizes scalpel-bougie-tube).
  • Do NOT release cricoid/laryngeal stabilization until bougie is securely inside tracheal lumen.
🏥 4. Ongoing Management
  • Secure airway tube mechanically (ties/sutures); maintain 100% oxygenation.
  • Maintain anesthesia and analgesia once oxygenation is re-established.
  • Infiltrate local anesthetic with adrenaline around stoma if not already done.
  • Arrange formal surgical tracheostomy or airway examination in theatre.
  • Postoperative ICU admission; comprehensive airway debrief, clinical incident documentation, and difficult airway registry notification.

Acute Hypermetabolic Emergency • MHAUS Protocol

Malignant Hyperthermia (MH)

Triggered by volatile anaesthetics (sevoflurane, desflurane, isoflurane) or succinylcholine. Acute hypermetabolic muscle state.

Source: Malignant Hyperthermia Association of the United States (MHAUS) • MHAUS Hotline: 1-800-644-9737
⚡ 1. Immediate Actions
  • Discontinue All Triggering Agents
    Turn off volatile vaporizers immediately; do NOT give succinylcholine. Maintain anesthesia with IV propofol/opioids.
  • Hyperventilate with 100% Oxygen
    High flow (≥10 L/min) at 2 to 3 times normal minute ventilation. Insert activated charcoal filters on inspiratory and expiratory limbs if available.
  • Call for MH Cart & Emergency Help
    Mobilize theatre staff to start reconstituting dantrolene immediately. Call MHAUS 24/7 Hotline: 1-800-644-9737.
  • Administer Dantrolene Immediately
    Initial dose 2.5 mg/kg IV based on ACTUAL body weight. Repeat as needed until signs resolve.

🚫 3. Critical Safety: What to Avoid
MHAUS Safety Warnings
  • AVOID CALCIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS (verapamil, diltiazem): May cause lethal cardiovascular collapse and severe hyperkalemia when combined with dantrolene.
  • DO NOT mix formulations: Use ONLY the specific formulation available in your hospital (Dantrium 20mg vs Ryanodex 250mg). Do NOT assume vial concentrations.
  • DO NOT use bacteriostatic water: Reconstitute ONLY with sterile water for injection WITHOUT bacteriostatic agents.
  • AVOID cold saline lavage once core temperature is <38.5°C: Stop active cooling to avoid hypothermia overshoot.
🩺 4. Supportive Management & Complications
Hyperkalemia (K > 5.9)
• Calcium Chloride: 10 mg/kg (up to 1,000 mg) OR Calcium Gluconate 30 mg/kg (up to 3,000 mg) IV.
• Insulin + Glucose: 10 units regular insulin + 50 mL 50% Dextrose (D50W).
• Sodium Bicarbonate: 1–2 mEq/kg IV.
Cooling Measures
• Infuse iced cold IV 0.9% saline (up to 1,000 mL/30 min).
• Apply ice packs to axillae, groin, and neck.
• STOP active cooling when core temperature <38.5°C (prevents hypothermia overshoot).
Acidosis & Hypercarbia
• Hyperventilate to normal PaCO2 with 100% O2.
• Sodium bicarbonate 1–2 mEq/kg IV for severe metabolic acidosis (guided by arterial blood gas / base deficit).
Renal Protection & Urine Output
• Target urine output > 1–2 mL/kg/h to prevent acute kidney injury from rhabdomyolysis/myoglobinuria.
• Hydration with IV crystalloid. Consider furosemide 0.5–1 mg/kg if needed.
Arrhythmia Management
• Correct hyperkalemia and acidosis first.
• Standard antiarrhythmics (amiodarone); NEVER give calcium channel blockers.
Laboratory Monitoring
• Serial ABG, electrolytes, lactate, CK, urine myoglobin, coagulation profile, core temperature.

ASRA Pain Medicine Checklist

LAST: Local Anaesthetic Systemic Toxicity

Acute central nervous system and cardiovascular toxicity following local anesthetic absorption or inadvertent intravascular injection.

Source: ASRA Pain Medicine Checklist for Treatment of LAST (2020 Update)
⚡ 1. Immediate Actions
  • Stop Local Anesthetic Injection Immediately
    Cease drug administration. Maintain sterile field if feasible.
  • Call for Help & 20% Lipid Emulsion Kit
    Alert resuscitation team, airway support, and alert cardiopulmonary bypass / ECMO team early.
  • Airway & Oxygenation (Prevent Acidosis / Hypoxia)
    100% O2 with hyperventilation. Hypoxia and acidosis dramatically worsen local anesthetic toxicity.
  • Suppress Seizures with Benzodiazepines
    Midazolam (1–2 mg IV titrations) preferred. Avoid large propofol doses in cardiovascular instability.

🚨 3. Critical: LAST Resuscitation vs Standard ACLS
LAST RESUSCITATION IS DIFFERENT FROM STANDARD ACLS
  • REDUCE EPINEPHRINE DOSES: Use small initial doses (≤1 mcg/kg or 10–100 mcg boluses in adults). Avoid standard 1 mg ACLS boluses, which impair lipid resuscitation and induce severe arrhythmias.
  • AVOID VASOPRESSIN: Vasopressin is contraindicated in LAST resuscitation.
  • AVOID CALCIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS & BETA-BLOCKERS: Both worsen local anesthetic myocardial depression and precipitate refractory bradycardia/asystole.
  • AVOID LOCAL ANESTHETIC ANTIARRHYTHMICS: Lidocaine, procainamide, and other class I sodium-channel blockers are strictly contraindicated (additive toxicity).
  • SEIZURE CONTROL: Benzodiazepines (midazolam) preferred. Propofol should be avoided if hemodynamics are compromised.
  • CARDIOPULMONARY BYPASS / ECMO: Alert perfusion and surgical teams immediately in severe or refractory arrest. Lipid rescue may require prolonged CPR until bypass is established.
⏱️ 4. Post-Resuscitation Observation
  • Minor neurotoxicity (seizure only): Minimum observation of 4 to 6 hours in a high-dependency setting.
  • Cardiovascular collapse / arrest: Minimum observation of 12 to 24 hours in intensive care (risk of delayed recurrent toxicity as lipid is cleared).
  • Do not discharge patient home on day of event.
  • Check for pancreatitis, fat embolism, or laboratory interference (lipemia affects hemoglobin, electrolytes).

Emergency Protocol • RCUK / AAGBI / NAP6

Perioperative Anaphylaxis

Severe, life-threatening generalized or systemic hypersensitivity reaction. Immediate trigger cessation, prompt adrenaline administration, aggressive fluid resuscitation, and serial mast cell tryptase sampling.

Source: Resuscitation Council UK & Association of Anaesthetists (AAGBI) Guidelines (NAP6)
⚡ 1. Immediate Life-Saving Actions
  • Stop Suspected Offending Agent Immediately
    Cease all infusions, antibiotics, neuromuscular blocking agents (e.g. rocuronium), chlorhexidine prep, colloids, blood products, or patent blue dye. Maintain surgical sterile field.
  • Call for Help & Alert Operating Room Team
    Announce "Anaphylaxis emergency" loudly. Request resuscitation trolley and cardiac arrest team if pulseless.
  • Airway & 100% High-Flow Oxygen
    Maintain airway patency. Intubate early if progressive angioedema or airway swelling occurs. Provide 100% FiO2.
  • Stop Volatile Agents & Elevate Legs
    Turn off volatile anaesthetics if hypotensive (switch to minimal TIVA / propofol or analgesia only). Elevate lower limbs or tilt table head-down (Trendelenburg) to preserve cerebral perfusion and venous return. Never sit patient up or move abruptly!

💊 3. Refractory Shock & Secondary Interventions

Refractory Vasoplegia (Adrenaline Resistant)

  • Noradrenaline infusion: 0.05 to 0.5 mcg/kg/min for profound vasoplegic vasodilatation.
  • Vasopressin: 1 to 2 units IV bolus, followed by 0.01 to 0.04 units/min infusion.
  • Patient on β-blockers: Administer Glucagon 1 to 2 mg IV over 5 minutes (Paeds: 20–30 mcg/kg, max 1 mg). Bypasses β-adrenergic receptors via direct adenylate cyclase activation.

Secondary Drugs (Non-Urgent)

⚠️ Secondary agents must NEVER delay adrenaline or rapid crystalloid administration!

  • Hydrocortisone: 200 mg IV (Paeds: 4 mg/kg, max 100 mg). Prevents biphasic/protracted reactions (onset 4–6 hours).
  • Chlorphenamine: 10 mg IV slow over 1 min (Paeds: 2.5–5 mg). Antihistamines do NOT reverse airway obstruction or shock.

Emergency Protocol • AAGBI / ASA Guidelines

Severe Intraoperative Bronchospasm

Acute expiratory airflow limitation and high airway pressures. Immediate exclusion of mechanical obstruction, anaesthetic deepening, stepwise inhaled and IV bronchodilator therapy, and ventilator de-trapping management.

Source: Association of Anaesthetists (AAGBI) & Difficult Airway Society Crisis Protocols
⚡ 1. Immediate Actions & Exclude Mechanical Causes
  • 100% Oxygen & Switch to Manual Bag Ventilation
    Assess compliance by hand — bag will feel stiff and non-compliant. Inspect capnography: classic shark-fin upsloping expiratory trace (prolonged phase II and loss of alveolar plateau).
  • Rule Out Endobronchial (Right Mainstem) Intubation
    Check tube depth at teeth/lips. Auscultate axillae bilaterally. Deflate cuff slightly or withdraw tube 1–2 cm if unverified.
  • Rule Out ETT Obstruction, Kinking, or Secretions
    Pass a suction catheter down the ETT. If suction passes easily, tube is patent; if blocked, tube is kinked, bitten, or plugged.
  • Rule Out Tension Pneumothorax & Anaphylaxis
    Check trachea position, percussion note, and blood pressure. Look for rash, urticaria, or profound vasodilation.

⚠️ 3. Ventilator Strategy & Breath Stacking Emergency

⚠️ Dynamic Hyperinflation (Auto-PEEP) Warning

In severe bronchospasm, air cannot escape during expiration. Each successive mechanical breath traps more gas inside the thorax ("breath stacking"). Intratoracic pressure skyrockets, compressing the vena cava and leading to sudden profound hypotension, PEA cardiac arrest, or tension pneumothorax.

Mechanical Ventilator Strategy
  • Low Respiratory Rate: 8 to 10 breaths/min.
  • Long Expiratory Time: I:E ratio 1:3 to 1:5 to allow complete passive lung deflation.
  • Permissive Hypercapnia: Accept PaCO2 50–70 mmHg provided pH ≥ 7.20.
  • Target Pplat < 30 cmH2O (plateau pressure reflects alveolar distension, while peak pressure reflects airway resistance).
The Circuit Disconnection Maneuver

If sudden severe hypotension or PEA occurs: IMMEDIATELY DISCONNECT THE ENDOTRACHEAL TUBE FROM THE CIRCUIT FOR 15–20 SECONDS and gently compress both sides of the chest. A loud rush of trapped air will decompress the chest, immediately restoring venous return and blood pressure.

Emergency Protocol • Airway Crisis

Laryngospasm: Acute Vocal Cord Spasm

Reflex involuntary closure of the vocal cords in response to stimulation in a light plane of anaesthesia. Immediate CPAP, Larson's notch pressure, propofol deepening, suxamethonium dosing, and negative pressure pulmonary oedema prevention.

Source: Difficult Airway Society (DAS) & Pediatric Airway Crisis Management Protocols
⚡ 1. Immediate Actions
  • Call for Help & Stop All Stimulation
    Halt surgical stimulation, drapes, suctioning, or extubation maneuvers immediately.
  • 100% Oxygen with Tight Two-Handed Mask Seal
    High-flow oxygen. Ensure an airtight mask seal to enable positive airway pressure generation.
  • Gentle Pharyngeal Suction (Under Direct Vision)
    Clear blood, secretions, or mucus from the posterior pharynx. Do NOT touch or poke the vocal cords or epiglottis!
👐 2. Essential Physical Maneuvers

Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP)

• Close the APL valve to 30 to 40 cmH2O.
• Squeeze reservoir bag gently to maintain 15–30 cmH2O continuous positive pressure.
• Splints the airway open, pushes vocal cords apart during expiration, and helps overcome soft tissue collapse.

Larson's Maneuver ("Laryngospasm Notch")

• Locate the "laryngospasm notch" behind each earlobe (between the mastoid process and ramus of mandible).
• Apply firm, steady inward and forward pressure with fingertips bilaterally while pulling the mandible forward.
• Intensely painful stimulation breaks the reflex arc of the vagus nerve and relaxes the laryngeal adductors.

🌊 4. Negative Pressure Pulmonary Oedema (NPPE) Safeguard

⚠️ Post-Extubation / Post-Spasm Complication

Vigorous inspiratory effort against closed vocal cords generates massive negative intrathoracic pressure (−50 to −100 cmH2O), causing transudation of hydrostatic fluid from pulmonary capillaries into the alveoli.

  • Signs: Pink frothy sputum in airway/mask, persistent tachypnoea, bilateral crackles, falling SpO2 despite resolution of spasm.
  • Treatment: Apply continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP / PEEP 5–10 cmH2O) with 100% O2. Maintain intubation and mechanical ventilation with PEEP if gas exchange is severely compromised.
  • Monitoring: Observe patient in high-dependency unit (PACU/HDU) for at least 4 to 6 hours. Obtain chest radiograph.
